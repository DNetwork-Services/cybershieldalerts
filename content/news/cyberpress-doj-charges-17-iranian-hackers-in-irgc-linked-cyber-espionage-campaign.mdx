The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) has unsealed a 14-count superseding indictment charging 17 alleged members and affiliates of the Iran-based Mabna Institute over a long-running cyber-espionage and data-theft operation allegedly conducted for the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and other Iranian government and university clients. The case expands the 2018 prosecution that initially named nine defendants. According to the DOJ, the alleged campaign began in 2013 and targeted a wide cross-section of academic, commercial, government, and non-governmental organizations worldwide. Victims included 144 U.S.-based universities, 178 foreign universities, at least 42 U.S. private-sector companies, 11 foreign companies, five U.S. federal and state agencies, and at least two NGOs. DOJ Charges 17 Iranian Hackers Investigators allege the group stole more than 31 terabytes of academic research, intellectual property, and proprietary information. The indictment describes a highly targeted spearphishing operation focused on university faculty. The alleged operators targeted more than 100,000 professor accounts globally and successfully compromised approximately 8,000 email accounts belonging to professors at institutions in the United States and countries including the UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, Japan, Israel, South Korea, and Singapore. Stolen credentials were allegedly used to access university systems and obtain scientific research, journals, theses, dissertations, e-books, and other academic resources spanning science, engineering, medicine, social sciences, and professional fields. The DOJ said the stolen material was exfiltrated to infrastructure outside the United States controlled by members of the conspiracy. U.S. universities alone had spent roughly $3.4 billion obtaining access to the targeted data and intellectual property. The government further alleges that the operation had a commercial component. Stolen academic resources were reportedly sold through Megapaper.ir and Gigapaper.ir, sites associated with defendant Abdollah Karima. Megapaper allegedly sold stolen resources to Iranian customers, including public universities, while Gigapaper reportedly enabled customers to use compromised professor credentials to access online university libraries directly. The Mabna-linked operators allegedly compromised employee email accounts at U.S. and foreign companies, government entities, and international organizations, including the Department of Labor, Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, the states of Hawaii and Indiana, the United Nations, and UNICEF. The superseding indictment also adds eight defendants and details attacks against private-sector organizations. Among them was HBO, where defendant Behzad Mesri had previously been charged over an intrusion involving stolen data and an alleged attempt to extort the company for approximately $6 million in Bitcoin. Other defendants are accused of using password-spraying attacks, reconnaissance, targeting list development, credential sharing, phishing message creation, and data exfiltration. The DOJ said that certain intrusions resulted in victims incurring more than $20 million in investigation and remediation costs. The defendants face charges including conspiracy to commit computer intrusions, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, unauthorized computer access for private financial gain, wire fraud, and aggravated identity theft. Maximum penalties vary by count, with wire fraud charges carrying up to 20 years in prison and aggravated identity theft carrying a mandatory 2-year sentence. The State Department’s Rewards for Justice program is offering up to $10 million for information leading to the location of Behzad Mesri, Mojtaba Galekuhi, Arman Kahzadian, Keyvan Fayaz, and Saber Shahbazi Ballojeh. The DOJ emphasized that the indictment contains allegations only, and all defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty.
