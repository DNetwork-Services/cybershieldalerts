A Chinese-speaking threat actor tracked as TA4922 is using tax-themed phishing campaigns to deploy PackClient, a modular remote access trojan (RAT) framework marketed through Telegram channels. Proofpoint researchers observed the group targeting organizations in mainland China and India during May and July 2026, using impersonated tax-authority notices to pressure recipients into opening malicious archives. The activity signals an expansion of TA4922’s initial-access toolkit and highlights the growing availability of sophisticated malware in Chinese-speaking cybercrime marketplaces. TA4922 Hackers Use Tax Phishing PackClient provides operators with data-theft, surveillance, remote control, payload delivery, and persistence capabilities, making it suitable for espionage, financial crime, reconnaissance, or follow-on ransomware operations. The first campaign, detected in late May, impersonated the Shandong Provincial Tax Bureau. Emails warned recipients that their organizations had been selected for a 2026 tax inspection and allegedly failed to pay stamp duties on purchase, sales, and lease contracts. Victims were directed to download a ZIP archive named 数据资料.zip from the actor-controlled domain gov12366[.]com. The archive contained an executable, 资料数据[.]exe, which initiated the PackClient infection chain. The malware’s first-stage downloader checks whether it has elevated privileges, drops a DLL such as xMain.dll, and invokes it through rundll32.exe. Tax Phishing (Source: proofpoint) It then retrieves an encrypted second-stage payload, decrypts it, saves it as a masqueraded executable commonly %TEMP%\svchost.exe and establishes RunOnce registry persistence. TA4922 later adapted the campaign for Indian targets. In mid-July, the group sent Hindi-language emails impersonating the Indian Income Tax Department. The messages accused victims of underreporting income or failing to disclose foreign assets, threatening financial penalties. Rather than directly delivering an executable, the emails used ZIP archives including Tax_Notice_23665.zip and ITDTAX202601987.zip, each containing an IMG disk-image file. Once mounted, the IMG files exposed an executable and a malicious DLL. The infection chain used DLL sideloading to launch Donut Loader, which ultimately installed PackClient. Proofpoint observed post-compromise connections to 64[.]81[.]30[.]99 in one campaign and to 192[.]252[.]180[.]45 over TCP port 6666 in another. In the latter case, the attackers deployed ManageEngine Remote Monitoring and Management software several hours after initial compromise, indicating an effort to establish broader remote access. PackClient consists of an initial downloader, a second-stage loader named PackClientLauncher, the PackClientCore RAT module, and downloadable plugins. The launcher retrieves and reflectively loads the core module into memory, while a separate --guard process monitors the RAT and restarts it if terminated. The core component supports two simultaneous C2 connections and accepts more than 60 commands. Its functions include shell execution, file system management, process enumeration, screenshot capture, webcam access, proxy tunneling, registry operations, browser data collection, keylogging, clipboard hijacking, and plugin installation. The malware also checks for Telegram Desktop and may support a plugin that modifies local Telegram configuration data, potentially enabling traffic interception. PackClient stores configuration data under HKCU\SOFTWARE\PackClientConsole\, including C2 hosts, ports, campaign identifiers, system UUIDs, and installation timestamps. Defenders should hunt for suspicious rundll32.exe executions loading DLLs from temporary directories, RunOnce entries launching %TEMP%\svchost.exe, the distinctive svchost.exe --guard process pattern, and unusual outbound TCP traffic on port 6666.
