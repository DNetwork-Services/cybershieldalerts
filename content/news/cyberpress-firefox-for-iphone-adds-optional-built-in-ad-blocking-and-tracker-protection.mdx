Mozilla has introduced an optional built-in Ad Blocker for Firefox on iOS, giving iPhone users a native way to reduce third-party advertising and related tracking activity while browsing. The feature, announced on September 1, 2026, is disabled by default and can be enabled through Firefox’s settings. The addition addresses a common mobile browsing problem: advertisements, pop-ups, overlays, and tracking scripts can consume limited screen space, slow page rendering, and interrupt access to content. Firefox for iPhone Adds Optional Built-In Ad Blocking Mozilla said the new option is designed to block many third-party ads and ad-related trackers before they load, reducing visual clutter and distractions on websites. Firefox’s iOS Ad Blocker uses Apple’s WebKit Content Blocker technology, the system-level framework that enables browsers and apps to apply content-filtering rules on iPhones and iPads. Mozilla has also integrated the widely used EasyList filter list to block advertising-related resources. Users do not need to download or configure a separate extension. The setting is available by opening Firefox, navigating to Settings> Browsing> Ad Blocker. Mozilla emphasized that the capability is optional, allowing users to decide whether ad blocking should be part of their mobile browsing experience. This distinction is significant for publishers and creators who depend on advertising income to support websites, journalism, and other online services. The built-in tool is not intended to remove every form of advertising. Mozilla said ads delivered directly by the website a user is visiting may continue to appear, as may paid placements displayed within search results. The feature also does not affect sponsored Firefox content, including sponsored shortcuts or other promotional material that may appear when users open a new tab. Those placements are separate from advertisements embedded in the websites users browse. This approach means the tool primarily targets third-party advertising infrastructure rather than all commercial content. It may also reduce the number of external ad-tech requests loaded by a webpage, potentially limiting exposure to tracking mechanisms associated with advertising networks. Mozilla said the Ad Blocker operates alongside Firefox’s existing privacy tools, including Enhanced Tracking Protection. That feature already blocks many known trackers and is designed to limit cross-site tracking activity. The new option expands Firefox’s privacy controls on iOS, where the browser&#8217;s capabilities differ from those on desktop and Android. Firefox users on desktop and Android can choose from a broad range of ad-blocking and privacy-focused extensions. Apple’s mobile operating system does not provide extensions in the same way, prompting Mozilla to build the functionality directly into Firefox for iPhone and iPad users. Mozilla framed the update as part of its wider effort to provide users more control over their browsing experience, including privacy settings, extensions on supported platforms, and controls governing AI features. Firefox users who encounter advertisements that should have been blocked, website compatibility issues, or unexpected behavior can report feedback through Mozilla Connect.
