Before you look at a single vendor, count the devices on your network that will never run an agent or hold a certificate printers, cameras, sensors, medical equipment, building systems, contractor laptops. If that number is large, agentless profiling quality is your primary criterion and Forescout or ClearPass lead. If it’s small, cheaper and simpler options become viable. Be honest about your deployment capacity. NAC has a reputation for failed projects, and the cause is almost always the same: enforcement was switched on before visibility was complete, and legitimate devices got locked out. Every platform here supports a monitor-only mode. Plan to run in it for weeks before enforcing anything, and choose a vendor whose deployment effort matches the team you actually have. Decide whether NAC is your segmentation layer. Some organizations use NAC purely as a gate authenticate and admit. Others use it to drive dynamic segmentation, assigning roles that determine reachable resources. The second approach is far more valuable and considerably harder, and it narrows your shortlist to Cisco ISE, ClearPass, Forescout, and Extreme. Check what happens when NAC fails. Ask each vendor directly what occurs if the policy server becomes unreachable. Fail-open leaves you unprotected; fail-closed can take a site offline. Understanding the failure mode and configuring it deliberately matters more than most feature comparisons. Common mistakes: buying NAC without also planning microsegmentation so policy stops at the point of admission; ignoring the guest and contractor workflow until after go-live; and shortlisting from a list containing duplicate vendors, as this one did. Situational FAQ What is network access control (NAC)? Network access control authenticates and profiles every device attempting to join a network, evaluates it against policy patch level, certificate, ownership, device type and then permits, restricts, or quarantines it. Modern NAC extends beyond 802.1X authentication to agentless profiling for the many devices that cannot authenticate themselves. Which NAC solution is best for a small business? Most enterprise NAC platforms are over-scoped for small businesses. OPSWAT MetaAccess and SafeConnect are the most accessible entries here, but many small organizations get better value from the access control built into their existing firewall or wireless platform, adding dedicated NAC only when device counts or compliance requirements demand it. Do I still need NAC if I have zero trust? Yes NAC is one of the enforcement points zero trust relies on. Zero trust removes implicit network trust; NAC is what actually inspects and admits a device at the network layer, particularly for IoT and OT devices that cannot participate in identity-aware application proxies. How long does a NAC deployment take? Realistically, several months for an enterprise deployment: discovery and profiling first, then policy design, then phased enforcement site by site. Cloud-delivered options can show value in weeks for endpoint compliance use cases, but full network enforcement with segmentation is a programme, not an installation. How much do NAC solutions cost? NAC is typically licensed per device or per concurrent endpoint, and enterprise platforms are quote-based. Cost scales with device count rather than user count, which surprises buyers in IoT-heavy environments where devices outnumber people several times over. Budget deployment services alongside licensing. Can NAC handle IoT and OT devices? Good NAC can, but not all NAC does. Devices that cannot run agents or supplicants require agentless profiling fingerprinting them by traffic behaviour, DHCP, and protocol signatures. Forescout leads here, with HPE Aruba ClearPass close behind. Platforms built primarily around 802.1X and endpoint agents struggle in these environments. The Short Version Match the platform to the problem, not to the market share. If unmanaged and operational devices dominate your network, Forescout is the clearest answer. If you’re a Cisco enterprise with the engineering depth to use it, Cisco ISE offers policy control nothing else matches. Campus and wireless-first organizations should look hard at HPE Aruba ClearPass, and mid-market teams wanting enforcement without a programme should start with OPSWAT MetaAccess. Whatever you choose, run in monitor mode until your device inventory is genuinely complete and check your shortlist for duplicate vendors before you start negotiating.
