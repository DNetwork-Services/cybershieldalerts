Microsoft has confirmed that LG has agreed to disable an unsolicited McAfee promotional pop-up delivered through its Monitor App Installer, following a wave of user complaints about the software silently arriving via Windows Update. The issue began when Reddit user Mags_Smash reported that Windows Update had silently installed the &#8220;LG Monitor App Installer&#8221; after connecting three LG UltraGear monitors, with a McAfee trial pop-up appearing shortly after without any consent screen. Windows Reliability Monitor and Event Viewer logs confirmed the installation originated from a background Windows Update process rather than any deliberate user action. Microsoft Gets LG to Disable McAfee Pop-Ups The story gained traction after reproducing the behavior on a $ 1,200 LG UltraGear 34GX900A-B monitor, triggering the McAfee pop-up on 31 of 32 consecutive boot cycles. The app&#8217;s own changelog listed McAfee as an &#8220;Additional App&#8221; bundled in a recent update alongside marketing copy for a 30-day free trial, contradicting LG&#8217;s later claims that installation required explicit consent. Public backlash pushed the LG Monitor App Installer&#8217;s rating down to a single star before the issue escalated further. The complaint reached Microsoft&#8217;s Windows leadership after Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney tagged Windows and Devices EVP Pavan Davuluri on July 19, 2026, noting reports of the behavior occurring &#8220;with arbitrary hardware&#8221;. Davuluri responded within hours, confirming his team was investigating, and by July 22 Microsoft had connected with LG to secure a fix. Davuluri&#8217;s official statement read: &#8220;Thanks again for bringing this to our attention. We&#8217;ve connected with the team at LG and as an immediate next step, they have agreed to disable the McAfee pop-up from their app. We appreciate LG working with us toward a shared goal of a better experience for our mutual customers. We will keep improving here with our ecosystem partners&#8221;. Thanks again for bringing this to our attention. This framing conflicts with user-recorded evidence and the app&#8217;s own changelog documentation. This incident highlights how Windows&#8217; device metadata pipeline lets OEMs push companion apps automatically when hardware is connected, a mechanism Microsoft&#8217;s own developer documentation warns can confuse users badly enough to damage app trust. Similar bootstrapping has occurred with other peripheral vendors, such as Razer&#8217;s camera drivers loading its Synapse software through the same channel, indicating this is not an isolated OEM behavior. Notably, macOS has no equivalent mechanism allowing a connected peripheral to trigger App Store installations.
