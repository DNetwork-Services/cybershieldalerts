ShinyHunters has publicly claimed responsibility for the Ernst & Young (EY) data breach, alleging it stole employee credentials and sensitive files through a supply-chain compromise of a third-party IT support platform. According to CSN, carries a &#8220;final warning&#8221; deadline of July 31, 2026, threatening to release all stolen data if EY does not negotiate before then. EY first disclosed the incident earlier this month after detecting anomalous activity on April 23, 2026, within an IT service management platform used by internal staff to support tax-related client work. Investigation revealed an unauthorized third party had access to the platform from March 28 to April 12, 2026, downloading documents tied to numerous EY clients during that roughly two-week window. ShinyHunters&#8217; Extortion Claim The compromised support tickets often contained attached client tax documents, exposing sensitive data including: Names and addresses Social Security numbers Financial account numbers Credit and debit card details Other information used to prepare tax filings EY filed breach notification letters with regulators including the California and Texas Attorneys General, confirming a floor of at least 1,366 affected residents across multiple US states, though the firm&#8217;s global client base suggests the real number is significantly higher. EY says it has no evidence of data misuse and doesn&#8217;t believe any client was individually targeted; it&#8217;s offering two years of free credit monitoring and identity restoration to affected individuals. Until this week, no threat actor had claimed the attack, and EY had not disclosed how intruders breached the third-party platform. EY Data Breach Claimed by ShinyHunters (Source: CSN) That changed when ShinyHunters listed EY on its leak site alongside new victims RingCentral and Brink&#8217;s Home, asserting the intrusion originated from a supply-chain attack that yielded credentials to EY&#8217;s internal systems. The group&#8217;s leak-site notice, updated July 27, 2026, warns: &#8220;This is a final warning to reach out by 31 July 2026 before we leak along with several ongoing (digital) problems&#8221; if EY fails to respond. This tactic mirrors ShinyHunters&#8217; established approach in recent campaigns against Instructure, Charter Communications, and McGraw Hill, where the group exploited SaaS platforms, SSO credentials, and vishing attacks to exfiltrate large data volumes before demanding ransom. ShinyHunters has emerged as one of 2026&#8217;s most prolific extortion operations, running a dedicated leak site and frequently exploiting third-party and supply-chain weaknesses rather than direct network intrusions. Recent victims tied to the group include Instructure&#8217;s Canvas LMS, affecting up to 275 million individuals, and Charter Communications, where 40 million records were allegedly taken via a compromised Microsoft Entra account and Salesforce instance. Security researchers note the group frequently overlaps with the &#8220;Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters&#8221; collective, which has also claimed attacks on firms like Abbott Laboratories using vishing and SaaS-platform exploitation techniques. As of this writing, EY has not confirmed ShinyHunters&#8217; specific claims or responded publicly to the July 31 deadline, and no stolen data has yet appeared on underground forums.
