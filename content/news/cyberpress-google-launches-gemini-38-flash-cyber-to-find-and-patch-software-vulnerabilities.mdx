Google has launched Gemini 3.8, a family of reasoning and coding models that includes Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, a specialized system built to help security teams discover software vulnerabilities and create working patches. The release arrives three weeks after Gemini 3.7 Flash and marks Google’s third Flash-tier launch in six weeks. Both Gemini 3.8 Flash and its Cyber counterpart share the same underlying architecture, but Google tuned them for distinct use cases. The general-purpose version focuses on long-horizon software engineering, agentic workflows, tool use, and debugging. Flash Cyber is instead directed toward defensive vulnerability research, validation, remediation, and code development. Google Launches Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber Google said Gemini 3.8 Flash improves software engineering performance while keeping its price at $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens. In DeepSWE v1.1, which measures end-to-end engineering work, Google said that the model outperformed larger frontier systems at a lower cost. It scored 54.9% on HLE-Verified. Gemini 3.8 Flash DeepSWE v1.1 Evaluation (Source: Google) Google attributed the improvements to a willingness to take additional reasoning steps and call tools repeatedly when a task requires investigation. Higher-effort settings can consume more tokens but may yield stronger results for planning, code review, troubleshooting, and multistage execution. This makes the model relevant for developers managing repositories. Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber is available only through Google’s Fairwind Program for vetted security teams. Google announced its approach to access in a research publication, citing the sensitivity of systems trained to identify exploitable weaknesses. Limiting distribution helps reduce the risk that discovery capabilities for vulnerabilities could be used for malicious purposes. Google said the model prioritizes defensive patching over exploitation capabilities. On CyberGym, an industry benchmark for vulnerability discovery, Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber reportedly surpassed Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber and larger frontier competitors. Google tested it against an internal benchmark covering twenty programming languages, extending beyond CyberGym’s C/C++ focus. In that internal evaluation, the model achieved a success rate exceeding 70%, representing a substantial improvement over earlier versions. Its coverage across multiple languages could be especially useful to enterprise defenders responsible for mixed software environments. Such environments often include legacy applications, cloud services, open-source components, and internally developed tools. Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber CWE-Bench Pass@1 vs. Cost per Rollout (Source: Google) On CWE-Bench, an external benchmark for automated software fixes run by Collinear, Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber recorded a 47.2% pass@1 score. Google said this nearly matched a leading frontier model’s 47.8% result while running at lower cost. The measurement indicates that patch quality can improve without requiring the largest models. Google said the model is already helping protect its own codebases. Chrome Security teams found that it produced 2.6 times as many correct vulnerability patches as its larger commercial rivals. Wiz separately measured 7.5% to 9.7% higher recall on internal penetration-testing benchmarks at two to five times lower cost. Google’s Cloud Vulnerability Research team used the model to uncover a foundational vulnerability in under two hours, according to the company. Google said similar discoveries can take months of manual investigation. By combining agentic reasoning with cybersecurity-specific training, Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber could give defenders ways to identify and remediate flaws.
